Investment objective

Absolute return (multi) strategies: Global Market neutral strategies aim to provide positive absolute returns regardless of the underlying market direction across a range of macroeconomic scenarios, mitigating exposure to market risk – the effect on an investment from the movement of the market as a whole.

Strategy

Sponsor led, Qualified & Accredited Own Capital (in-house) & global Third-Party (external) institutional investment LP capital assets are managed by Advisory (ERA) & globally based teams spreading capital across portfolios, with assets held on low volatility portfolios growing by an average of 14% (to achieve full funding) whilst Advisory assets rise by an estimated 18% YoY. Adding a 20% alternatives allocation (55% Equities & 25% FI 'traditional' allocation) to Market Neutral portfolio construction helps maintain an attractive and comparative return profile whilst reducing volatility and maximum drawdown. LP assets are applied in the accumulation (growth stage) & decumulation (LP generated net income drawdown) phases. See Player International Fund for the full return objectives and compartment strategies.

Risks

All investments are subject to market risk, including the possible loss of principal. The growth compartment's approach could cause it to underperform other stock funds employing a different investment style; the income compartment's emphasis on dividend-paying stocks could cause it to underperform similar funds investing without consideration of a company's dividend track record; the BDC portfolio's large- and mid-cap holdings tend to be less volatile than small-cap securities but may not attain the same high growth rates.

Optimal portfolio weights, rebalancing & capital calls

Market timing: skillful market timing produces an excess return equal to 0.40%, with excess risk of 0.80%. We derive these values by simulating a market-timing strategy's performance.

Rebalancing: we simulate five-year paths given assumptions about each portfolio's allocation as well as the expected return, risk and correlations for each asset class, comparing an annual rebalance (at a cost of 0.2%) against no rebalance, and compute the illiquidity penalty as the difference between the resulting certainty equivalents.

Capital calls: we assume a 10% probability LPs will need to meet capital calls in a given month, and that calls are likely to be no greater than 15% of initial portfolio value, to sustain liquid AuM inflows and portfolio optimality across asset classes.

LP capital calls & maintaining liquidity

  • As long as cumulative excess capital calls are below 15% of initial portfolio value, we draw on the liquid assets (proportionally) to meet capital calls.
  • When cumulative excess capital calls exceed 15% of initial portfolio value, LPs begin internal adjustment to meet capital calls, at a rate of 5%. We do not allow cumulative excess capital calls to exceed 15% of initial portfolio value.
  • Using the same multiple paths from the rebalancing simulation (fund level VaR & CVaR & MV simulations), we take the difference between the certainty equivalents of two scenarios: one in which we remove capital calls proportionally from all assets and rebalance annually, and another in which we remove capital calls only from the liquid assets and do not rebalance.
  • We deploy liquidity (incorporating liquidity into portfolio choice) to restore optimality, drawing on a 'shadow' LP non-portfolio interests as a unifying model to deliver expected returns.

Our model mirrors what actually occurs within a portfolio; allows LP investors to map liquidity onto units of expected return and risk; treats absolute and partial illiquidity within a single, unifying framework; and accounts for the fact that liquidity serves not only to meet demands for capital, but also to exploit trading opportunities.

Income compartment

Key features: the income focused 'Dedicated Portfolio' compartment offers return consistency & the ability to meet LP cash-flow & capital return requirements (liability based fixed income mandates), with portfolio construction following two main approaches of cash flow matching & duration matching to match fixed-income assets with multiple LP future liabilities.

Methodology: our balanced portfolio approach is mindful of implicit biases, risks and trade-offs that could arise with portfolio measures, instruments (publicly traded securities such as commercial paper, notes and bonds traded through exchanges as well as OTC, and non-publicly traded alternative FI instruments such as loans and private placements), and special purpose vehicles used in fixed-income portfolio management. Compartment asset–liability management (ALM) strategies incorporate both rate-sensitive assets and liabilities into the portfolio decision-making process; for liquidity stress testing (interest rate, liquidity & credit risk) the compartment LDI ALM framework incorporates an 'immunization' feature to hedge interest rate risk to cover cash flow & duration matching techniques across economic/market cycles.

Player International Inc. – private credit & the loan trust

Private Credit funds are organised as non-traded BDCs, into which LP investors seeking the highest possible levels of investment income can move regulated capital asset base into (and out of) at par value, through BDC debt series supporting non-cyclical Direct, Wholesale & Dedicated Assets structured lending & simultaneous credit line drawdowns for the proportion of assets funded by debt.

Acting by Hamilton Portfolio Holdings Trust ("HAMTRUST") Treasury operations, external wholesale funding and BDC capital (equity tranches) helps reinforce our Private Markets activities and mitigate adverse scenarios using hedging approaches to achieve efficient & sustainable portfolio outcomes. HAMTRUST funds all liability (debt & obligations) originated by Hamilton Corporate Finance by borrowing internally (from Private Credit business lines) as well as externally (from wholesale funding markets) and assumes the associated liquidity, currency and interest rate risks.

Together with HAMTRUST cash surplus distributions

  • Cash interest and dividends (Equity Series).
  • Net Interest Income (Debt Series). Owing to our structure, we have a nil concentration of PIKs, representing less than 1 per cent of individual debt series BDC gross investment income. ("PIK interest" refers to payment-in-kind, where a borrower pays interest by adding it to the loan's principal balance instead of making a cash payment to the lender.) We have a credit risk policy to mitigate in-cycle loan interest accrual, compounding and default. At origination, our process does not structure loans as PIKs; in-cycle, we may amend after origination to relieve liquidity pressure in a specific situation, rather than structured at origination as PIKs (i.e. junior debt is repaid after more senior debt in the event of a borrower's insolvency). We mitigate credit losses & defaults using a bespoke risk management process.
  • Maintains Collateral in the wholesale debt market, in the form of eligible collateral for initial & ongoing hedging margin using cash surpluses generated by HAMTRUST.
  • Recycles cash generated either from loans maturing or being redeemed early (in a falling spread environment we forecast a third of loans either mature or get prepaid by their borrowers who find alternative refinance).
  • Uses new shareholder subscriptions & capital calls to grow Direct Lending and Wholesale Lending capacity, subject to achieving strong performance (measured against the S&P BDC Index) and maintaining nominal credit losses below 1%, thereby maintaining satisfactory borrowing headroom ("Dry Powder") via HAMTRUST Treasury operations.

Underwriting, selection & pricing

Maintaining strong credit statistics and portfolio returns by taking a consistent approach to spreads on new loan originations, to maintain an overall portfolio spread of 8.75% (+/- 0.5%) over Daily Simple SOFR (SOFR + 875bps), when pricing floating rate notes, bonds and derivatives:

  • Direct portfolio: Treasury Bills (T-bills), a short-term government interest rate used as a benchmark; and CPI (Consumer Price Index), a measure of inflation used as a reference rate for some securities.
  • Wholesale lending origination: Prime Rate, a rate commonly used for adjustable-rate mortgages, calculated for the Mortgages origination operations.

Assuming improved borrower collateral quality or where the price of credit drops, HAMTRUST has the choice of re-originating the current DL portfolio at a reduced rate % over SOFR. The surplus cash may either be distributed to holders or retained as additional margining capital. By design, the construction of the loan portfolio serves to maintain diverse and strong credit selection and high recovery rates, sustaining return on equity consistency for BDC shareholders in a falling interest rate environment. While SOFR does not precisely equal the fed funds rate, it moves pretty much lock step with the fed funds rate. The BDC & HAMTRUST take a non-leveraged exposure approach to the private credit market, thus mitigating credit portfolio systemic risk. Each BDC Debt & Equity Series has structured the manager's performance fee to come from investment income rather than total returns, taking the S&P BDC Index as debt series benchmark, directly incentivising managers to optimize investment income, aligned with credit outcome (maintaining a low credit loss & default strategy) and maintaining shareholder return consistency required to maintain the BDC preference dividends and strong periodic 'bonus dividend' distributions generated from Loan portfolio surpluses achieved from positive impacts from credit environment sensitivities and in-cycle spread compression.

Collateral

Private Credit transactions are typically collateral backed, thereby typically mitigating credit risk. Transaction 'character', capital (or collateral), and capacity make up the three C's of credit assessment. Credit history, sufficient finances for repayment, and collateral are all factors in establishing private credit facilities with borrowers.

Overall framework: collateralized transactions. A collateralized transaction is one in which private credit funds have a credit exposure or potential credit exposure, and that exposure is hedged in whole or in part by collateral posted to HAMTRUST by a counterparty or by a third party on behalf of the counterparty. Where HAMTRUST takes eligible financial collateral, it is allowed to reduce the credit exposure to a counterparty when calculating capital requirements, to take account of the risk-mitigating effect of the collateral.

  • Collateral pledging & transfer: HAMTRUST aligns with the legal mechanism by which collateral is pledged or transferred to ensure that the lending private credit fund has the right to liquidate or take legal possession of it, in a timely manner, in the event of default, insolvency or bankruptcy of the counterparty. HAMTRUST, acting by its Trustees, takes all steps necessary to fulfil those requirements under the law applicable to the private credit fund's interest in the collateral, for obtaining and maintaining an enforceable security interest.
  • Collateral quality: in order for collateral to provide protection, the credit quality of the counterparty and the value of the collateral must not have a material positive correlation.
  • Timely liquidation: HAMTRUST has clear and robust procedures for the timely liquidation of collateral, to ensure that any legal conditions required for declaring the default of the counterparty and liquidating the collateral are observed, and that collateral can be liquidated promptly.
  • Collateral, escrow & security agents: where the collateral is held by a custodian, HAMTRUST & Hamilton PR (Pty) Ltd T/A Hamilton Fund Services (as administrator), together take reasonable steps to ensure that the custodian segregates the collateral from its own assets.

HAMTRUST posts two main types of margin: initial margin, a buffer posted upfront to cover initial risk, and variation margin, a daily or periodic payment to account for mark-to-market losses as the contract's value fluctuates. Collateralization is governed by agreements such as the ISDA Credit Support Annex (CSA). The value of collateral is calculated daily (or more frequently) by a designated calculation agent; if the derivative's value changes, additional collateral is posted or returned to keep the exposure fully covered. Cleared derivatives follow the rules of the relevant central clearing counterparty (CCP); uncleared OTC derivatives follow regulations such as EMIR to reduce counterparty risk.

Our approach monitors Portfolio at Risk (PAR), a financial metric used to assess the credit risk of the lender's loan portfolio, indicating the proportion of the loan portfolio at risk of default or late payment for each private credit portfolio set. Note: collateral reuse (rehypothecation) can be posted with the right for the collateral-taker to use it to manage liquidity; however, initial margin is generally forbidden from being reused.

Funds transfer pricing (FTP)

Funds Transfer Pricing is a process Player Fund Inc. uses to assign liquidity costs, benefits, and risks to internal business units, managed by HAMTRUST in accord with the central Treasury function & Hamilton Corporate Finance. This internal pricing mechanism charges units consuming liquidity (e.g. lending) and credits units providing liquidity (i.e. Pref Capital deposits). The FTP process is designed to cover both the asset and liability sides of the balance sheet, in order to centralize risk and align all product pricing with market rates.

Functions & group mechanisms

  • Centralized Treasury: HAMTRUST acts as the internal hub, managing the pool of debt series (Pref Capital) funds. Player International GP SARL serves as the Treasurer for Player International Inc.
  • Internal pricing: HAMTRUST charges business units that require funds (assets/loans) for the cost of liquidity and pays units that provide funds (liabilities/deposits) for the benefit they bring.
  • Funding collateral & hedging: for transactional risk management, HAMTRUST further calculates derivatives pricing and continuously monitors collateral & pricing variations for margining & funding valuation adjustments.
  • Performance measurement: by incorporating liquidity costs and benefits into product pricing and performance evaluations, business units (via Hamilton Corporate Finance) have their profitability accurately reflect the true economic cost and benefit of their activities related to liquidity.

Key benefits

  • Accurate product pricing: ensures that the full costs and benefits of a product, including liquidity, are reflected in its final price.
  • Improved risk management: aligns the risk-taking incentives of individual business lines with the liquidity risks they create for the group as a whole.
  • Improved resource allocation: encourages the efficient management and allocation of liquidity by treating it as a scarce and costly resource.
  • Transparent incentives: creates transparent and understandable incentives for business units to manage liquidity prudently.

FTP is a core component of sound liquidity risk management, as emphasized by regulatory bodies including the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision and the Bank of England. We align with regulatory expectations to incorporate liquidity costs and benefits into all significant business activities and internal processes.
